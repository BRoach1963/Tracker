# Effective 1:1 Meetings: A Manager's Guide

One-on-one meetings are the most important tool a manager has for building relationships, developing team members, and ensuring alignment. This guide covers why 1:1s matter and how to run them effectively.

## What Is a 1:1 Meeting?

A one-on-one (1:1) meeting is a dedicated, recurring conversation between a manager and their direct report. Unlike team meetings or project updates, 1:1s are focused on the individual—their growth, challenges, feedback, and career.

### What 1:1s Are NOT

- ❌ Status update meetings
- ❌ Project reviews
- ❌ Performance evaluations
- ❌ A time for the manager to give orders
- ❌ Optional when things are busy

### What 1:1s ARE

- ✅ The employee's meeting (their agenda first)
- ✅ A safe space for candid conversation
- ✅ Relationship building time
- ✅ Career development discussions
- ✅ Early problem detection
- ✅ Feedback exchange (both directions)

## Why 1:1s Matter

### 1. Build Trust
Regular 1:1s create psychological safety. When employees know they have dedicated time with their manager, they're more likely to raise concerns before they become problems.

### 2. Improve Retention
Employees don't leave companies; they leave managers. Strong 1:1 relationships are one of the best retention tools available.

### 3. Accelerate Development
Career conversations happen in 1:1s. Without dedicated time for growth discussions, development becomes an afterthought.

### 4. Catch Problems Early
Issues surface in 1:1s before they escalate. A weekly check-in catches problems that monthly meetings miss.

### 5. Increase Engagement
Employees who have regular 1:1s with their manager are significantly more engaged than those who don't.

## 1:1 Cadence

### Recommended Frequency

| Situation | Frequency |
|-----------|-----------|
| New employees | Weekly (first 3-6 months) |
| Standard direct reports | Weekly or bi-weekly |
| Senior/autonomous staff | Bi-weekly to monthly |
| Remote team members | Weekly (minimum) |
| During difficult periods | Weekly or more |

### Duration

- **30 minutes minimum**: Allows meaningful conversation
- **45-60 minutes ideal**: Gives space for depth
- **90+ minutes**: For quarterly career conversations

### Consistency Matters

**Don't cancel 1:1s.** Reschedule if needed, but canceling sends a message that the employee isn't a priority. Protect this time.

## 1:1 Structure

### A Sample Format

| Time | Topic |
|------|-------|
| 5 min | Check-in & connection |
| 15 min | Employee's agenda |
| 10 min | Manager's topics |
| 10 min | Development & forward-looking |

### The Employee Sets the Agenda

The 1:1 belongs to the direct report. They should come with topics to discuss. If they consistently have nothing, that's a signal—either they don't feel safe sharing, or they need coaching on how to use the time.

### Manager's Role

- **Listen more than talk** (aim for 70/30 or better)
- **Ask questions** rather than give answers
- **Take notes** and follow up on commitments
- **Be present** (no phones, no multitasking)

## 1:1 Topics

### Regular Check-ins

**The Basics** (quick pulse):
- How are you doing?
- What's top of mind this week?
- Any blockers I can help with?

**Work Discussion**:
- What's going well?
- What's challenging?
- Where do you need support?
- What feedback do you have for me?

### Career Development

**Growth**:
- What skills do you want to develop?
- Where do you see yourself in 1-2 years?
- What projects would stretch you?
- What's holding you back?

**Aspirations**:
- What does success look like for you?
- What kind of work energizes you?
- What would your ideal role look like?

### Relationship & Feedback

**Bidirectional**:
- What could I do differently to support you better?
- Is there anything I'm doing that's not helpful?
- How do you prefer to receive feedback?
- What do you need more or less of from me?

### Team & Organization

**Broader context**:
- How are things on the team?
- Any concerns about team dynamics?
- What's the mood in your area?
- Questions about company direction?

## Questions That Open Conversation

### To Understand How They're Doing

- "What's giving you energy lately?"
- "What's draining you?"
- "How are you feeling about your workload?"
- "What's been on your mind?"

### To Identify Challenges

- "What's the hardest part of your job right now?"
- "Where are you stuck?"
- "What would make your work easier?"
- "What's frustrating you?"

### To Encourage Reflection

- "What have you learned recently?"
- "What would you do differently if you could?"
- "What are you most proud of?"
- "What surprised you this week?"

### To Support Growth

- "What skill would have the biggest impact for you?"
- "What do you want to be known for?"
- "Who do you admire and why?"
- "What would make you excited to come to work?"

### To Get Feedback

- "What could I do better as your manager?"
- "What information do you wish you had?"
- "Am I giving you enough/too much direction?"
- "What's one thing I should start/stop/continue?"

## Common 1:1 Mistakes

### 1. Making It a Status Update
If you spend the whole time on project updates, you're missing the point. Save status for other forums.

### 2. Doing All the Talking
The manager shouldn't dominate. If you're talking more than 30% of the time, step back.

### 3. Canceling Frequently
Consistent cancellations signal that the employee isn't important. Reschedule instead of canceling.

### 4. Not Preparing
Both parties should come prepared. As a manager, review previous notes and think about what you want to discuss.

### 5. Avoiding Difficult Topics
1:1s are the right place for tough conversations. Don't let issues fester.

### 6. No Follow-Up
If you commit to something, follow through. Track action items and close the loop.

### 7. Same Conversation Every Week
Mix it up. Not every 1:1 needs the same format. Go deep on career monthly, do quick pulse checks weekly.

## Running Effective 1:1s

### Before the Meeting

**Manager**:
- Review notes from last 1:1
- Check on any commitments you made
- Note any feedback or observations
- Prepare questions, not monologues

**Direct Report**:
- Come with agenda items
- Think about challenges and wins
- Prepare questions or concerns
- Be ready to share honestly

### During the Meeting

**Opening**:
- Start with genuine human connection
- Ask open-ended questions
- Let them set the direction

**Middle**:
- Listen actively
- Ask follow-up questions
- Probe deeper when appropriate
- Take notes on key points

**Closing**:
- Summarize commitments
- Clarify next steps
- Check if anything else is on their mind

### After the Meeting

- Review and organize notes
- Complete any immediate action items
- Follow up on commitments promptly
- Prepare for the next 1:1

## 1:1 Meeting Notes

### What to Capture

- Key discussion points
- Commitments made (both sides)
- Follow-up items
- Career development notes
- Feedback shared
- Concerns or risks mentioned

### Why Notes Matter

- Shows you're paying attention
- Enables follow-up
- Tracks patterns over time
- Documents career conversations
- Helps with performance reviews

### Privacy Considerations

1:1 notes are confidential. Store them securely and only share what's appropriate with HR or others when necessary.

## Remote & Hybrid 1:1s

### Camera On
Video matters for connection. Seeing faces builds relationships that audio alone cannot.

### Minimize Distractions
Close other apps, silence notifications, and give full attention.

### Extra Check-ins
Remote workers need more frequent touchpoints. Weekly is minimum; consider brief mid-week check-ins.

### Talk About More Than Work
Isolation is real for remote workers. Make space for personal connection.

### Watch for Signals
It's harder to read people remotely. Ask directly about well-being more often.

## When Things Are Difficult

### If They're Struggling
- Show empathy first
- Understand the root cause
- Offer support before solutions
- Check in more frequently
- Document concerns appropriately

### If There's Conflict
- Address it directly but kindly
- Listen to understand their perspective
- Focus on behaviors, not personality
- Agree on path forward together

### If Trust Is Broken
- Acknowledge the issue
- Own your part
- Rebuild incrementally
- Demonstrate consistency over time

---

*The quality of your 1:1 meetings largely determines the quality of your management. Invest the time, be present, and make these conversations count.*
