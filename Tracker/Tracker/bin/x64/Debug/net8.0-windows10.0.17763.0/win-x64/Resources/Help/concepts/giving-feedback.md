# Giving Effective Feedback

Feedback is one of the most powerful tools for developing people. Done well, it accelerates growth and strengthens relationships. Done poorly, it damages trust and performance. This guide covers how to give feedback that actually helps.

## Why Feedback Matters

### For Individuals
- Accelerates learning and growth
- Clarifies expectations
- Reinforces positive behaviors
- Corrects blind spots
- Builds confidence when done well

### For Teams
- Creates a culture of continuous improvement
- Reduces surprises in performance reviews
- Builds trust through candor
- Improves collective performance
- Encourages risk-taking and innovation

### For Organizations
- Develops talent pipeline
- Improves retention (people crave growth)
- Increases performance across the board
- Creates competitive advantage

## Types of Feedback

### Positive Feedback (Reinforcement)
Acknowledges what someone did well to encourage repetition.

**Purpose**: "Do more of this"

**Example**: "The way you handled that difficult customer call was impressive. You stayed calm, acknowledged their frustration, and found a creative solution. That's exactly the approach we need."

### Constructive Feedback (Redirection)
Addresses behaviors or outcomes that need to change.

**Purpose**: "Do this differently"

**Example**: "I noticed the project update didn't include the risk section we discussed. That information is critical for stakeholder decisions. Can you add that for next week's report?"

### Appreciation
Expresses gratitude and recognition.

**Purpose**: "Thank you—I see you"

**Example**: "I really appreciate you staying late to help the team meet the deadline. Your commitment made a difference, and I want you to know it didn't go unnoticed."

## The SBI Model

The Situation-Behavior-Impact model is the most effective framework for clear, actionable feedback.

### S - Situation
Describe when and where the behavior occurred. Be specific.

**Examples**:
- "In yesterday's team meeting..."
- "During your presentation to the client on Tuesday..."
- "When you responded to Sarah's question in Slack..."

### B - Behavior
Describe the observable behavior—what you saw or heard. No interpretation.

**Do**: "You interrupted twice during the discussion"
**Don't**: "You were rude and disrespectful"

### I - Impact
Explain the effect of the behavior on you, the team, or the outcome.

**Examples**:
- "...which made it hard for others to share their ideas"
- "...which built the client's confidence in our expertise"
- "...which delayed the decision by a week"

### SBI Examples

**Positive**:
"In the client call yesterday (S), you anticipated their questions and came prepared with data to support each point (B). The client commented afterward that they felt confident in our expertise, and they approved the proposal on the spot (I)."

**Constructive**:
"During the sprint planning meeting (S), you checked your phone several times while others were presenting (B). It gave the impression you weren't engaged, and I noticed team members seemed reluctant to share ideas after that (I)."

## Giving Constructive Feedback

### The Right Mindset

**Before you give feedback, ask yourself**:
- Is my intention to help them improve? (Not to vent or punish)
- Am I basing this on observed behavior? (Not assumptions or rumors)
- Is this timely? (Ideally within days, not months)
- Is this the right setting? (Private for constructive feedback)

### Key Principles

**1. Be Specific**
- ❌ "You need to communicate better"
- ✅ "In the last three status emails, key deadline changes were buried in the third paragraph. Moving them to the top would help stakeholders act faster."

**2. Focus on Behavior, Not Character**
- ❌ "You're careless"
- ✅ "The last two reports had data errors in the summary tables"

**3. Be Timely**
Feedback loses power over time. Aim to deliver within 24-48 hours of the behavior.

**4. Be Direct**
Don't bury the message. Be clear about what needs to change.

**5. Invite Dialogue**
Ask for their perspective. You might be missing context.

**6. Agree on Next Steps**
End with a clear path forward. What will they do differently?

### The Feedback Conversation

**Step 1: Set Context**
"I want to share some feedback about the presentation yesterday. Is now a good time?"

**Step 2: Describe What Happened (SBI)**
Share the Situation, Behavior, and Impact clearly.

**Step 3: Invite Their Perspective**
"How do you see it?" or "What was going on from your side?"

**Step 4: Discuss Together**
Listen, understand their context, and work toward shared understanding.

**Step 5: Agree on Path Forward**
"What will you do differently next time?" or "How can I support you?"

**Step 6: Express Confidence**
"I'm confident you can improve this. I've seen you handle difficult situations before."

## Giving Positive Feedback

Positive feedback is just as important as constructive—and often under-delivered.

### Common Mistakes

- **Too generic**: "Good job" doesn't help them know what to repeat
- **Too rare**: Don't save it all for annual reviews
- **Private only**: Public recognition (when appropriate) amplifies impact
- **Followed by "but..."**: Undermines the positive with criticism

### Effective Positive Feedback

**Be Specific**:
- ❌ "Nice work on the project"
- ✅ "Your stakeholder analysis identified risks we would have missed. The risk register you created is being used as a template for other projects."

**Be Timely**:
Immediate recognition is most powerful. Don't wait.

**Explain the Impact**:
Help them understand WHY it mattered.

**Make It Personal**:
Connect it to their specific strengths or growth areas.

## Receiving Feedback

Great feedback cultures require people who can receive feedback well.

### In the Moment

1. **Listen fully** - Don't interrupt or get defensive
2. **Assume positive intent** - They're trying to help
3. **Ask clarifying questions** - "Can you give me an example?"
4. **Thank them** - Even if you disagree, thank them for sharing
5. **Reflect before responding** - It's okay to say "Let me think about that"

### After the Conversation

1. **Consider it honestly** - Is there truth here?
2. **Seek other perspectives** - Does this pattern show up elsewhere?
3. **Decide what to act on** - Not all feedback is valid, but most contains some truth
4. **Follow up** - Let them know what you're doing with the feedback

### If You Disagree

- Don't dismiss it immediately
- Share your perspective calmly
- Look for the kernel of truth
- Agree to disagree if needed, but don't get defensive

## Creating a Feedback Culture

### For Managers

- **Model receiving feedback well** - Ask for feedback on yourself
- **Give feedback frequently** - Not just in reviews
- **Make it normal** - Reduce the stakes by doing it regularly
- **Celebrate growth** - Acknowledge when people improve

### For Teams

- **Establish norms** - Agree how the team will give and receive feedback
- **Practice** - Use role-playing or peer feedback exercises
- **Debrief projects** - Build feedback into retrospectives
- **Appreciate publicly** - Create moments for positive recognition

## Common Feedback Mistakes

### 1. The Feedback Sandwich
**Don't**: Positive → Negative → Positive
**Why**: People learn to distrust your positives

**Instead**: Be direct. Separate positive and constructive feedback.

### 2. Vague Feedback
**Don't**: "You need to be more strategic"
**Why**: Impossible to act on

**Instead**: Give specific examples and clear expectations.

### 3. Character Attacks
**Don't**: "You're not a team player"
**Why**: Attacks identity, creates defensiveness

**Instead**: Focus on specific behaviors and their impact.

### 4. Feedback via Email
**Don't**: Deliver constructive feedback in writing (unless remote and scheduled for call)
**Why**: Loses nuance, can be misread, no dialogue

**Instead**: Have the conversation in person or video call.

### 5. Public Criticism
**Don't**: Give constructive feedback in front of others
**Why**: Creates shame and resentment

**Instead**: Praise publicly, criticize privately.

### 6. Delayed Feedback
**Don't**: Wait months to address an issue
**Why**: Loses context and relevance

**Instead**: Give feedback within 24-48 hours.

### 7. No Follow-Up
**Don't**: Give feedback and forget about it
**Why**: No accountability for change

**Instead**: Check in on progress in subsequent 1:1s.

## Documentation

### When to Document

- Ongoing performance issues
- Formal performance conversations
- Significant positive achievements
- Career development discussions
- Any feedback that may become part of a pattern

### How to Document

- Record facts, not interpretations
- Note specific behaviors and dates
- Include context and impact
- Document the conversation outcome
- Store securely and confidentially

---

*Feedback is a gift. Given with care, it helps people become the best versions of themselves. The best managers are constant feedback machines—not because they're critical, but because they're invested in their people's growth.*
