# Metrics and Goals Surfacing Strategy

This document defines how **metrics** and **goals** should appear across the three primary product surfaces:

- **Circle** – shared context
- **Me** – personal ownership and meaning
- **Briefing** – time-scoped action

The intent is to prevent duplication, reduce cognitive overload, and ensure that metrics and goals *change behavior*, not just display information.

---

## 1. Core Mental Models

Clear mental models are non-negotiable. If these blur, everything downstream becomes confusing.

### Circle

**Question it answers:**
> How are *we* doing?

Circle is the shared, team-level context.

- Emphasizes alignment
- Shows collective progress
- Makes ownership visible

Circle is about *state*, not personal action planning.

---

### Me

**Question it answers:**
> What do *I* own, and what does it mean over time?

Me is the user’s persistent personal workspace.

- Reflective
- Ownership-focused
- Long-lived

Me is where goals and metrics gain *personal meaning*.

---

### Briefing

**Question it answers:**
> What needs my attention *right now*?

Briefing is:
- Time-scoped
- Action-oriented
- Ephemeral

Briefing is not a dashboard. It is a work queue.

---

## 2. Where Metrics and Goals Belong

### Circle: Yes, Fully

Metrics and goals naturally belong in Circle.

**What belongs here:**
- Team or group goals
- Metrics tied to shared objectives
- Progress over time
- Visibility into ownership and accountability

**What does not:**
- Personal scratchpad goals
- Private or exploratory metrics
- Individual micro-tracking

Circle answers:
> Are we moving in the right direction, together?

---

### Me: Yes, but Framed Differently

Goals and metrics should appear in Me, but **not as a clone of Circle**.

Me is about *relationship*, not reporting.

#### Goals in Me

Include:
- Goals the user owns
- Goals the user contributes to
- Goals assigned to the user
- Goals the user explicitly follows

Goals should be framed around:
- Status
- Next action
- Risk or confidence
- Notes and reflection
- Linked tasks, meetings, and feedback

Avoid:
- Raw status dashboards
- Team-level comparisons
- Manager-style reporting

---

#### Metrics in Me

Metrics should appear **only if they affect the user directly**.

Include:
- Metrics the user owns
- Metrics the user is accountable for
- Metrics directly tied to the user’s goals

Present metrics as:
- Current state vs expectation
- Trend (up / down / flat)
- Interpretation ("Is action required?")

Avoid:
- Full historical charts
- Peer comparisons
- Raw numbers without meaning

Me answers:
> What do these goals and metrics mean for *me*, and what should I do about them?

---

### Briefing: Only When They Create Work

Metrics and goals do **not** automatically belong in Briefing.

They belong **only when they trigger action**.

Briefing should never display static metrics or goal lists.

---

## 3. Rules for Promoting Goals or Metrics into Briefing

A goal or metric may appear in Briefing **only if one or more of the following is true**:

### Trigger Conditions

- A metric crosses a defined threshold
- A metric trend turns negative or stalls
- A goal is off-track or at risk
- A deadline or checkpoint is approaching
- A review, update, or check-in is due

If none of these are true, the item stays out of Briefing.

---

### How Items Appear in Briefing

When promoted, the item must be:

- Short
- Interpreted
- Actionable

**Good examples:**
- "Customer satisfaction dropped below target → review feedback"
- "Q2 hiring goal at risk → update plan before Friday"
- "Weekly metrics review due today"

**Bad examples:**
- "Sales conversion: 3.4%"
- "Goal progress: 62%"
- "Revenue metric (FYTD)"

Briefing answers:
> What needs my attention *today* because of goals or metrics?

---

## 4. Transformation Across Surfaces (This Is the Key)

Metrics and goals flow through the system, but they **must be transformed** at each surface.

- Circle shows **state**
- Me shows **ownership and meaning**
- Briefing shows **urgency and action**

They should never look identical in two places.

---

## 5. Guardrails

- If a metric does not change what the user should *do*, it does not belong in Briefing
- If a goal does not belong to the user personally, it does not belong in Me
- If it is not shared, it does not belong in Circle

These rules prevent duplication, overload, and confusion.

---

## 6. Summary

Used correctly:

- Circle provides alignment
- Me provides meaning
- Briefing provides focus

Metrics and goals are valuable only when they inform decisions.

This structure ensures they do exactly that.
