# Cohere – Goals: Concept and UI (Initial Specification)

This document defines the foundational concept and user interface for Goals in Cohere. Goals replace traditional OKRs and are designed to be narrative-first, discussion-driven, and manager-friendly. This document intentionally excludes lifecycle and AI behavior, which will be defined in subsequent specifications.

## 1. Core Goal Philosophy

Goals represent intent and direction. They answer the question: 'What are we trying to achieve?' Goals are not tasks, checklists, or numeric commitments.

Metrics provide signals that inform goals but never define them. Progress is assessed through conversation, reflection, and trend awareness.

## 2. Goals vs Metrics

Goals:
- Express intent and focus
- Are owned by individuals or teams
- Evolve over time
- Are discussed in meetings

Metrics:
- Measure observable signals
- May span multiple goals
- Are never pass/fail indicators
- Support narrative decision-making

## 3. Goal Types (Conceptual)

Cohere supports multiple goal types to match how managers think:

Growth Goals – Personal development and capability building
Execution Goals – Concrete outcomes and delivery focus
Operational Health Goals – Stability and sustainability
Directional / Exploratory Goals – Learning and assessment

Goal types inform conversation context but do not impose structure.

## 4. How Goals Interact with Meetings

Goals are first-class, discussable objects in meetings.

- Goals can drive agenda items
- Agenda items may generate goals only through explicit user action
- Goals are refined, reframed, or supported through meeting outcomes

Goals are never automatically created by the system or AI.

## 5. Goal List UI

The goal list view is designed for scanning, not analysis.

Goals are grouped by scope:
- My Goals
- Team Goals
- Shared Goals

Each goal row displays:
- Goal title
- Owner
- Qualitative health
- Subtle metric trend indicator (optional)
- Last discussed indicator

## 6. Goal Detail UI

The goal detail view emphasizes clarity and history over metrics.

Primary sections include:
- Goal statement and ownership
- Health status with reflective reason
- Associated metrics (collapsed by default)
- Activity and discussion history
- Supporting tasks
- Notes and reflections

## 7. Health Instead of Scoring

Goal health replaces OKR-style scoring.

Health is qualitative and manager-owned, such as:
- On track
- Needs attention
- At risk
- Reframing needed

Health changes prompt reflection but do not enforce numeric thresholds.

## 8. What the Goal UI Avoids

To preserve trust and usability, the Goal UI explicitly avoids:

- Percent completion
- Numeric grades or confidence scores
- Progress bars
- Quarterly reset pressure
- Metric-driven pass/fail judgments

## 9. Guiding Principle

Goals should feel like conversations you are responsible for, not numbers you are accountable to.
